#### Exhaustion
**Tag:** Condition
**Source:** p365

**Exhaustion Levels.** This condition is cumulative. Each time you receive it, you gain 1 Exhaustion level. You die if your Exhaustion level is 6.

**D20 Tests Affected.** When you make a D20 Test the roll is reduced by 2 times your Exhaustion level.

**DCs Reduced.** Your spell save DC and the DCs of your abilities are reduced by 2 times your Exhaustion level.

**Speed Reduced.** Your Speed is reduced by a number of feet equal to 5 times your Exhaustion level.

**Removing Exhaustion Levels.** Finishing a Long Rest removes a number of your Exhaustion levels equal to half your Constitution modifier (rounded up, minimum 1), provided that you drink and eat. When your Exhaustion level reaches 0, the condition ends.

#### Long Rest
**Tag:** Rule
**Also:** Long Rests

A Long Rest is a period of extended downtime—at least 8 hours—available to any creature. During a Long Rest, you sleep for at least 6 hours and perform no more than 2 hours of light activity, such as reading, talking, eating, or standing watch.

During sleep, you have the Unconscious condition. After you finish a Long Rest, you must wait at least 16 hours before starting another one.

**Benefits of the Rest.** To start a Long Rest, you must have at least 1 Hit Point. When you finish the rest, you gain the following benefits:

**Regain All HP.** You regain all lost Hit Points and all spent Hit Point Dice. If your Hit Point maximum was reduced, it returns to normal.

**Ability Scores Restored.** If any of your ability scores were reduced, they return to normal.

**Exhaustion Reduced.** If you have the Exhaustion condition, its level decreases by half your Constitution modifier (rounded up, minimum 1), provided that you drink and eat.

**Special Feature.** Some features are recharged by a Long Rest. If you have such a feature, it recharges in the way specified in its description.

**Interrupting the Rest.** A Long Rest is stopped by the following interruptions:

- Rolling Initiative
- Casting a spell other than a cantrip
- Taking any damage
- 1 hour of walking or other physical exertion

If you rested at least 1 hour before the interruption, you gain the benefits of a Short Rest.

You can resume a Long Rest immediately after an interruption. If you do so, the rest requires 1 additional hour per interruption to finish.

#### Short Rest
**Tag:** Rule
**Also:** Short Rests, Short

A Short Rest is a 1-hour period of downtime, during which a creature does nothing more strenuous than reading, talking, eating, or standing watch. To start a Short Rest, you must have at least 1 Hit Point.

**Benefits of the Rest.** When you finish the rest, you gain the following benefits:

**Spend Hit Point Dice.** You can spend one or more of your Hit Point Dice to regain Hit Points. For each Hit Point Die you spend in this way, roll the die and add your Constitution modifier to it. You regain Hit Points equal to the total (minimum of 1 Hit Point). You can decide to spend an additional Hit Point Die after each roll.

**Special Feature.** Some features are recharged by a Short Rest. If you have such a feature, it recharges in the way specified in its description.

**Interrupting the Rest.** A Short Rest is stopped by the following interruptions:

- Rolling Initiative
- Casting a spell other than a cantrip
- Taking any damage

An interrupted Short Rest confers no benefits. A character can’t benefit from more than two natural short rests in a 24-hour period, and a character must have at least 1 hit point at the start of the rest to gain its benefits. Short rest effects created by spells (such as *Prayer of Healing* or *Catnap*) don't count towards this limit.

#### Circle Magic
**Tag:** Rule
**Also:** Circle Spell, Circle Spells

**Overview**

Circle Magic is a collaborative spellcasting technique developed by the elves of Faerûn and later spread across Thay and Rashemen. By joining their arcane energies in a ritual circle, multiple casters can amplify a spell far beyond what any individual could achieve alone.

**The Two Traditions**

Circle magic has been practiced across Faerûn for millennia, and two distinct traditions have emerged, each reflecting a different philosophy of how power should flow within a circle.

**The Hierarchical Tradition.** Practiced by formally trained arcane orders, military spellcasting units, and any circle where one caster commands the others, the hierarchical tradition places absolute authority in the hands of the primary caster. Secondary casters offer their power to the circle, but the primary caster has final authority over how that power is shaped — they may change any secondary caster's chosen contribution to a different one of their choice before the spell takes effect, regardless of what that caster intended. The secondary caster's spell slot is still expended. Each secondary caster's chosen contribution is conveyed wordlessly through the Weave to the primary caster alone — only the primary caster knows the full shape of what the circle has offered.

**The Communal Tradition.** Practiced by druidic circles, religious congregations, and any group of equals pooling their magic together, the communal tradition holds that every voice in the circle is sovereign. No single caster may redirect another's contribution — once a secondary caster chooses their contribution and takes the Magic action, that choice is final and cannot be altered by anyone else. Each secondary caster's chosen contribution is conveyed wordlessly through the Weave to every member of the circle. Through this same connection, any caster in the circle may silently request that another change their chosen contribution before they act — but each caster remains free to ignore such a request. No member of a communal circle can be compelled.

**What Is a Circle Spell?**

When a group of spellcasters collectively channel their magic into a single spell, they can enhance and augment the spell's magic in powerful ways. A spell cast in this way is known as a Circle spell.

**Eligible Spells**

Any spell that has a casting time of an action or 1 minute or longer and is cast using a spell slot can be cast as a Circle spell. To cast a Circle spell, follow the usual rules for spellcasting, as described in the Player's Handbook, as well as the following rules for Circle spells.

**Casting a Circle Spell**

A Circle spell has one primary caster ("you" in these rules). Unless otherwise specified, you as the primary caster decide the spell's targets, maintain Concentration if required by the spell's Duration entry, provide the spell's components, expend the slot for casting the spell, and decide any of the other options noted in the spell's description. The spell's effects originate from you.

**Initiating a Circle Spell**

You take a Magic action to initiate casting a Circle spell. Until the Circle spell's casting is complete (see below), you must maintain Concentration on the spell.

If a Reaction would trigger when a creature casts a spell — such as the Reaction taken to cast Counterspell — it also triggers when you take this action to initiate a Circle spell.

**Secondary Casters**

Casting a Circle spell requires that one or more other spellcasters, called secondary casters, lend their magic to the spell. A secondary caster must have either the Spellcasting or Pact Magic feature to participate in a Circle spell.

After you initiate the spell, each secondary caster takes the Magic action to contribute to the spell. A secondary caster can take this action only while within 30 feet of you and before the start of your next turn.

Each secondary caster chooses one option from the Circle Casting Options list (see below) and expends the spell slot required by that option when they take the Magic action.

**NPC Secondary Casters**

Some Circle spells require secondary casters to expend one or more spell slots to participate in the spell's casting. At the DM's discretion, NPCs capable of casting spells — such as spellcasters hired in settlements — can meet this requirement by expending one or more limited uses of any spells of the same level or higher in place of the required spell slots.

**Completing the Casting**

If the spell has a casting time of an action, the Circle spell's effects occur immediately after the final secondary caster takes the required action to contribute to the spell. You decide which secondary caster is the final one.

If the spell has a casting time of 1 minute or more, you and each secondary caster must take the Magic action on each of your turns for the entire casting time, and you must each maintain Concentration while you do so. In this case, if any caster's Concentration is broken, the spell fails.

**Spell Failure**

If a Circle spell fails, nothing happens. You don't expend a spell slot, and any Material components aren't consumed. To cast the spell again, you must start over.

**Circle Casting Options**

When a secondary caster contributes to a Circle spell, they choose one of the following options and expend the required spell slot. Multiple secondary casters may choose the same option (effects stack as described) or each may choose a different one. The tradition of the circle determines whether the primary caster may override those choices (see The Two Traditions).

**Augment**

*You extend the caster's reach, lending your senses and power to carry the spell across vast distances.*

**Spell Slot Required:** Any level.

**Effect:** The spell's range increases by 200 feet per level of the spell slot you expend. The maximum total range increase from Augment across all secondary casters is 1 mile (5,280 feet).

Additionally, the first secondary caster who chooses Augment grants the primary caster a special awareness of the spell's extended reach: when targeting a creature or point at a range made possible only by Augment, the primary caster doesn't suffer disadvantage on spell attack rolls due to being unable to clearly see or perceive the target, provided at least one caster in the circle can see or sense the target.

*This option has no effect on spells with a range of Self or Touch.*

**Distribute**

*You share the mental burden of the spell, weaving your concentration into the circle alongside the primary caster's.*

**Spell Slot Required:** A spell slot of a level equal to the level of the spell being cast.

**Effect:** Only one secondary caster needs to choose this option for the full effect to activate; additional secondary casters choosing Distribute provide no further benefit.

When Distribute is active, all casters who contributed to this casting — including the primary caster — jointly maintain Concentration on the spell. Each of them is subject to the normal rules for Concentration, and each must maintain it independently. The spell persists as long as at least one of them maintains Concentration; it ends only when every contributing caster has lost Concentration.

*Choose this option carefully — it limits how many other Concentration spells your circle members can maintain simultaneously.*

**Expand**

*You pour your magic into the edges of the spell, pushing its boundaries outward.*

**Spell Slot Required:** Any level.

**Effect:** The spell's area of effect increases by 5 feet in one chosen dimension per level of the spell slot you expend. Each secondary caster who chooses Expand independently selects which dimension to extend. If two secondary casters both choose Expand, they may target the same dimension (stacking their extensions) or different dimensions.

The expandable dimension depends on the spell's area type: the radius for a Sphere, Cylinder, or Emanation; the length for a Cone; the width or length (your choice) for a Line; or any one side length for a Cube.

*For spells that create multiple discrete areas, each Expand contribution affects one dimension of one of those areas, not all of them simultaneously.*

**Prolong**

*You sustain the weave of the spell, pouring your own magical reserves into extending its duration.*

**Spell Slot Required:** Any level.

**Effect:** The spell's duration is extended based on the level of the spell slot you expend. Multiple secondary casters who choose Prolong add their extensions together.

| Spell Slot Level | Duration Added |
|---|---|
| 1st | +1 minute |
| 2nd | +10 minutes |
| 3rd | +1 hour |
| 4th | +4 hours |
| 5th or higher | +8 hours |

*This option only affects spells with a base duration of 1 minute or longer. It has no effect on instantaneous spells or spells with a duration measured in rounds.*

Prolonging a spell does not remove its Concentration requirement. A spell that requires Concentration still does so for the full extended duration, unless Distribute is also active.

**Safeguard**

*You carve a pocket of safety into the spell's destructive geometry, protecting those within.*

**Spell Slot Required:** Any level.

**Effect:** The primary caster designates a number of 5-foot cubes of safe space within the spell's area of effect equal to 3 times the level of the spell slot you expend. Creatures and objects entirely within a designated cube are unaffected by the spell. Multiple secondary casters who choose Safeguard add their cubes together.

Safeguard cubes must be placed within the spell's area of effect at the time of casting and do not move. If a creature moves out of a cube after the spell takes effect, it is subject to the spell's effects normally.

*This option has no effect on spells that do not create an area of effect.*

**Supplant**

*You offer your own magical essence in place of rare and costly components.*

**Spell Slot Required:** Any level.

**Effect:** You reduce the gold cost of one of the spell's material components by 50 gp per level of the spell slot you expend. If the reduction brings the component's cost to 0 gp or below, the component is not required for this casting, and if the spell would normally consume that component, it is not consumed.

Multiple secondary casters may choose Supplant, stacking their reductions on the same component or applying them to different components if the spell has more than one costly material requirement.

*Supplant cannot replace components that serve a specific narrative purpose, such as unique named objects or quest items. It applies to material costs only.*

**Summary**

| Option | Slot Required | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Augment** | Any | +200 ft range per slot level (max 1 mile); first caster removes extreme-range perception penalty |
| **Distribute** | Equal to spell level | Shares Concentration across all contributors (one caster is enough to unlock) |
| **Expand** | Any | +5 ft per slot level to one chosen dimension of the area of effect |
| **Prolong** | Any | +1 min / +10 min / +1 hr / +4 hrs / +8 hrs based on slot level |

| **Supplant** | Any | Reduces one material component's cost by 50 gp per slot level |

#### Crafting Nonmagical Items
**Tag:** Rule
**Also:** Crafting Mundane Items

**Resources and Resolution**. In addition to the appropriate tools for the item to be crafted, a character needs raw materials worth half of the item’s selling cost. A character can spend money to collect raw materials which weigh 1 pound for every 50 gp spend. The item has a *time cost* equal to its selling price in gp. If its time cost is covered, the item is created.To determine how many days (working 8 hours a day) it takes to make an item, divide its purchase cost in GP by 10 (round a fraction up to a day). For example, you need 5 days to make a Heavy Crossbow, which sells for 50 GP. Then roll an Intelligence check using the appropriate tool and consult the **Final Crafting Time** table. This roll represents the cumulative effort of many days of work. Only bonuses that are permanent, always available and require no action to maintain apply to this roll - effects that must be actively cast or triggered, such as Bardic Inspiration or Guidance, do not, unless they explicitly state otherwise. A character cannot work on item crafting for more than 8 hours a day, unless he succeeds on an 15 appropriate ability check to extend this limit by 2 hours. For every time they roll to extend the limit before they finish a long rest, the DC increases by 5. A character can pause crafting an item and resume its crafting at any time, as long as the work in progress is stored in a safe location.

Multiple characters can combine their efforts. Every helping character makes an Intelligence (Tool) check and adds the result minus 10 to the main crafting character's roll. Use your judgment when determining how many characters can collaborate on an item. A particularly tiny item, like a ring, might allow only one or two workers, whereas a large, complex item might allow four or more workers.

A character needs to be proficient with the tools needed to craft an item and have access to the appropriate equipment. Everyone who collaborates needs to have the appropriate tool proficiency. You need to make any judgment calls regarding whether a character has the correct equipment.

If all the above requirements are met, the result of the process is an item of the desired sort.

**Final Crafting Time**

| Intelligence (Tool) Check Roll | Final Time Required |
|:----:|:-------------|
| 1-4 | You spend the basic time required, only to ruin the item. Half the raw materials are lost. |
| 5-9 | You spend two times the basic time required to make the item |
| 10-14 | You spend one and a half times the basic time required to make the item |
| 15-19 | You spend the basic time required to make the item |
| 20-24 | You spend half the basic time required to make the item |
|25-29|You spend one third the basic time required to make the item|
|30-34| You spend one fourth the basic time required to make the item|
|35+| You spend one fifth the basic time required to make the item|

#### Hiding
**Tag:** Rule
**Link:** yes
**Also:** Hidden

Being hidden means you have successfully concealed your presence from one or more creatures. The environment, shadows, or distractions might conceal you, allowing you to move undetected through dangerous areas or gain the upper hand in combat.

**Hidden**

- A creature from which you are Hidden does not know your current location, but can recall where you were when you became Hidden if it was aware of you at that time.
- You have advantage on attack rolls against a creature from which you are Hidden.
- Attacking while Hidden reveals your position to all creatures in the vicinity.
- Your position is also revealed to any creature that can see you, if you make an audible sound, or if you otherwise reveal where you are (such as by leaving footprints in snow or mud, disturbing dust, or disrupting the environment).

**Moving While Hidden**

- You can move up to half your speed without making any sound and without requiring a Stealth check.
- To move faster than half your speed while remaining silent, you must make a Dexterity (Stealth) check. You remain Hidden from creatures whose passive Wisdom (Perception) you beat, but reveal your position to those you don't.
- Using the Dash action while Hidden produces audible noise and reveals your position.

**Becoming Hidden**

To Hide, you must not be clearly visible to those you are trying to hide from. Make a Dexterity (Stealth) check. You become Hidden from each creature whose passive Wisdom (Perception) score is less than your check result. Your DM may require you to make a new Stealth check if circumstances change, such as when you move close to enemies or across difficult terrain.

**Finding Hidden Creatures**

A creature can take the Search action on its turn to make a Wisdom (Perception) check to detect your presence, contested by your Stealth check.

A creature that is aware of your location can attempt to inform allies who are not aware, if able to communicate with them.

**Invisibility**

An invisible creature is impossible to see without the aid of magic or a special sense. For the purpose of hiding, the creature is heavily obscured. The creature's location can be detected by any noise it makes or any tracks it leaves.

**Invisibility and Hiding**

Being invisible is distinct from being hidden. Invisible creatures cannot be seen, but their location might still be detectable through sounds they make, tracks they leave, or other environmental disturbances. An invisible creature must still take the Hide action to become Hidden.

**Finding an Invisible and Hidden Creature**

- Creatures might use other senses such as hearing, smell, or special senses such as blindsight to detect your presence.

#### Lingering Injuries
**Tag:** Rule
**Also:** Lingering Injury

It's up to you to decide when to check for a lingering injury. Consider calling for this check after particularly brutal blows, boss fights, or moments where a lasting consequence would add drama; not as a matter of routine after every knockdown. A creature has an 25% chance of sustaining a lingering injury under the following circumstances:

- When it drops to 0 hit points but isn't killed outright.
- When it fails a death saving throw by 5 or more.
- Fails a saving throw against a damaging effect by rolling an 1.
- When it is the target of a critical hit.

To determine the nature of the injury, determine the type of damage that triggered the injury and roll on the appropriate table below that corresponds to the damage type. If multiple types of damage caused the injury, use the type that dealt the majority of damage, or randomly determine the damage type to use when you roll for the lingering injury.

#### Bonus and Penalty dice
**Tag:** Rule

When a creature rolls a d20 and rolls additional dice as a penatly or bonus to the roll, only the greatest die applies, with each additional die affecting the roll giving a +1 to the result of the die. For example, if a creature makes an attack roll and has a 1d8 bardic inspiration, is under the effect of the *Bless* spell, and under the effects of the Emboldening Bond of the Peace Domain cleric, the creature rolls 1d8+2. Similarly, if it is under the effects of the Bane spell and an enemy Wild Magic Sorcerer uses Bend Luck to give a penatly to the attack roll, you roll 1d4+1 to apply as a penalty.

#### Combat Bluffing
**Tag:** Rule

When you take the Attack action on your turn, you can replace one attack with an attempt to deceive one humanoid you can see within 5 feet of you. Make a Charisma (Deception) check opposed by the target's Wisdom (Insight) check. If your check succeeds, your movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks from the target and your attack rolls against it have advantage; both benefits last until the end of your next turn or until you use this ability on a different target. If your check fails, the target can't be deceived by you in this way for 1 hour.

#### Counterspelling Counterspells
**Tag:** Rule

If a character counterspells a counterspell, they must make an ability check using their Spellcasting ability modifier to avoid magic going awry. The DC equals 10 + the level of the counterspell counterspelled. On a failure, the character rolls on the Wild Magic Table.

#### Deadly Critical hits
**Tag:** Rule

Using this rule, if you land a critical hit, you do not roll all of the attack’s damage dice twice and add them together. Instead, your attack deals maximum damage and you also roll the attack's damage dice normally, and add those two amounts together. For example, a critical hit with a longsword deals 1d8+8+str modifier damage. Additionally, the creature can forgo rolling the dice (dealing only maximum damage) to inflict a random lingering injury.

#### Dextrous Jumping
**Tag:** Rule

You can calculate your Jumping distance using your Dexterity modifier, instead of your strength modifier, if you have a feature that allows you to make unarmed strikes using your Dexterity modifier.

#### Distracting Performance
**Tag:** Rule

While performing, you can try to distract one humanoid you can see who can see and hear you. Make a Charisma (Performance) check contested by the humanoid's Wisdom (Insight) check. If your check succeeds, you grab the humanoid's attention enough that it makes Wisdom (Perception) and Intelligence (Investigation) checks with disadvantage until you stop performing.

#### Drowning and Suffocating
**Tag:** Rule

These drowning rules aim for a more realistic underwater fight:

- You have a number of "breath points" equal to 10 + 10 times your Constitution modifier (minimum of 5).
- Each time you take an action, bonus action or reaction, you lose 1 breath point. If you cast a spell with verbal components, you lose all breath points.
- At the start of your turn, you lose 1 breath point if you don’t have access to air, or regain an amount of breath points equal to 5 + your Constitution modifier if you have access to air.
- At the start of your turn, you lose 1 additional breath point if you have taken damage at any point after your previous turn.
- When you suffer a critical hit, you must make a Constitution saving throw to keep holding your breath. The DC equals 10 or half the damage you take, whichever is higher. If you fail, you lose all your breath points.

- If your breath points get below 0, you must succeed on a DC 10 + the negative number of breath points Constitution saving throw or drop to 0 hit points and start dying at the start of your next turn. For example, if you have -3 breath points, the DC for the save is 13.

#### Identifying Spells
**Tag:** Rule

Sometimes a character wants to identify a spell that someone else is casting or that was already cast. To do so, a character can use their reaction to identify a spell as it's being cast, or they can use an action on their turn to identify a spell by its effect after it is cast.

If the character perceived the casting, the spell's effect, or both, the character can make an Intelligence (Arcana) check with the reaction or action. The DC equals 15 + the spell's level. If the spell is cast as a class spell and the character is a member of that class, the check is made with advantage. If the character beats the DC by 5 or more, they don't spend their action or reaction.

#### Spellcasting Focuses and Somatic Components
**Tag:** Rule

A character can perform the somatic components of spells with a hand that holds a spellcasting focus, even if the spell in question does not have material components. The character is then considered to having used the focus for that casting.

#### Spell Effects on Equipment
**Tag:** Rule

With this rule, all spells or abilities that make a weapon or armor magical work on weapons and armors that are already magical. If two or more effects on an item confer the same type of bonus, only the greatest bonus applies. For example, casting *magic weapon* on a *dagger of venom* has no effect because it already has a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls. Casting *magic weapon* on it as a 4th level spell, confers the +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls, as the greatest bonus applies.

#### Spellcasting Restrictions
**Tag:** Rule

**Casting in Armor**

A spell of 1st level or higher gained through a class may only be cast with armor or shield equipped if that class (or its subclass) provides the training for that armor or shield, or if that training is a result of a feat taken as part of that class. This rule supersedes Laserllama's class features that disallow casting in armor.

**One Spell with a Spell Slot per Turn**

On a turn, you can expend only one spell slot to cast a spell. This rule means you can't, for example, cast a spell with a spell slot using the Magic action and another one using a Bonus Action on the same turn.

#### Using another Wizard's Spellbook
**Tag:** Rule

If a wizard finds a spellbook, there is no need to copy every one of them into their own spellbook. Instead, they can spend 8 hours per spell level of the highest spell level present in the spellbook and then make an Intelligence (Arcana) ability check. The DC equals 10 + the spell level of the highest spell level present in the spellbook. On a success, they treat it as their own.

#### Using Spell Scrolls
**Tag:** Rule

Using this rule, anyone can try to use a spell scroll, even if the spell on the scroll is not on their spell list.

- If the spell is on the Cleric or Paladin spell list, you must make an Intelligence (Religion) check.
- If the spell is on the Wizard, Sorcerer or Warlock spell list, you must make an Intelligence (Arcana) check.
- If the spell is on the Druid or Ranger spell list, you must make an Intelligence (Nature) check.
- If the spell is on multiple spell lists, you choose which roll to make.

If the spell is not on your class's spell list, the DC equals 10 + two times the spell’s level. If the spell is on your class's spell list and of a level higher than you can cast, the DC is 10 + the spell's level, and you make an ability check using your Spellcasting Ability with the corresponding skill, instead of Intelligence. As normal, casting a spell from your spell list requires no roll, if the spell is of a level you can cast.

A creature who tries and fails to cast a spell from a spell scroll must make a DC 10 Intelligence saving throw. If the saving throw fails, roll on the Scroll Mishap table. If the saving throw succeeds, the creature can choose one effect from the Scroll Mishap table or nothing to happen.

**Scroll Mishap**

|d6| Result|
|:----:|:-------------|
|1| A surge of magical energy deals the caster 1d6 force damage per level of the spell.|
|2| The spell affects the caster or an ally (determined randomly) instead of the intended target, or it affects a random target nearby if the caster was the intended target.|
|3| The spell affects a random location within the spell’s range.|
|4| The caster suffers a minor but bizarre effect related to the spell. Such effects last only as long as the original spell’s duration, or 1d10 minutes for spells that take effect instantaneously. For example, a fireball might cause smoke to billow from the caster’s ears for 1d10 minutes.|
|5| The spell’s effect is contrary to its normal one, but neither harmful nor beneficial. For instance, a fireball might produce an area of harmless cold.|
|6| The spell activates after 1d12 hours. If the caster was the intended target, the spell takes effect normally. If the caster was not the intended target, the spell goes off in the general direction of the intended target, up to the spell’s maximum range, if the target has moved away.|

#### Weightless Worn Armor
**Tag:** Rule

While worn, armor does not count toward a character's carrying capacity. Armor that is carried but not worn weighs normally.

#### Choosing Languages
**Tag:** Language Note

Your character begins play knowing at least two languages: One language from the Standard Languages Table, and one language from either the Standard or Rare Languages tables. The character knows additional languages equal to their Intelligence ability score modifier at 1st level. A character does not learn languages retroactively when they raise their Intelligence ability score modifier.
