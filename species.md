### Choose a Species
In the following pages of this book, you can find all the sapient species that inhabit the world of Dungeons and Dragons.

When you choose your character’s Species, you determine whether your character is human or of a fantastical Species, such as Dragonborn or Gnome. This chapter provides descriptions and rules for many Species options. The peoples of the D&D multiverse hail from many worlds and are members of many different sapient lifeforms.

A player character’s Species is the set of game traits that an adventurer gains from being one of those life-forms. A character’s upbringing and training are represented in the rules by Class and Background. Some Species can trace their origin to a single world, plane of existence, or god, while other Species first appeared in multiple realms at once. Whatever a Species’ origin story, its members have spread across the multiverse and contribute to various cultures.

The description of each Species includes a section of game traits, which your character gains if you select that Species.

#### Creature Type
Every creature in D&D, including every player character, has a special tag in the rules that identifies the type of creature it is. Most player characters are of the Humanoid type. Here’s a list of the game’s creature types in alphabetical order:
Aberration, Beast, Celestial, Construct, Dragon, Elemental, Fey, Fiend, Giant, Humanoid, Monstrosity, Ooze, Plant, Undead.

These types don’t have rules themselves, but some rules in the game affect creatures of certain types in different ways. For example, the description of the Charm Person spell specifies that its target must be a Humanoid.

#### Children of Different Species
Thanks to the magical workings of the multiverse, sapient species of different kinds sometimes have children together. For example, folk who have a human parent and an orc or an elf parent are particularly common. Many other combinations are possible. If you’d like to play the child of such a wondrous pairing, choose two Species options to represent your parents. Then you can choose your size and traits from the size and traits of either of your parents' species, but those traits must have a combined trait points value between 17 and 19. Each trait has its trait point value listed next to its name. If both parent species share a trait, you must choose this trait. You can then mix and match visual characteristics—color, ear shape, and the like—of the two options. For example, if your character has a halfling and a gnome parent, you can decide that your character has the pointed ears that are characteristic of a gnome. Finally, determine the average of the two options’ Life Span traits to figure out how long your character might live. For example, a child of a halfling and a gnome has an average lifespan of 288 years. A species that has no trait value next to any of its traits means that it cannot breed with other species.

#### Intraspecific Spellcasting
Some species allow you to cast one or more spells once per day, without using a spell slot. When you do so, that instance of spell does not count as a spell of 1st level or higher for the purpose of casting another spell using a bonus action, and it does not require any material components when you cast it without using a spell slot.

### Customizing Ability Scores
You have 27 points to spend on your ability scores. The cost of each score is shown on the Ability Score Point Cost table. For example, a score of 14 costs 7 points. Using this method, 15 is the highest ability score you can end up with, before applying racial increases. You can't have a score lower than 7 or higher than 15.

### Build Your Background

Using the rules here, you can build a Background from scratch or customize a premade Background, focusing on details related to the backstory you have in mind for your character.

When you build a Background, your character gains the features in the “Background Features” section below. As you make choices for those features, think about your character’s past. Where did they spend most of their time? What did they do for a living? What capabilities and possessions did they acquire? What language did they learn from their family, associates, or studies? How did their past affect their ability scores?

If you instead decide to customize a premade Background, you can choose any features in that Background and replace them with the features below of the same name. For example, if you want to change a Background’s Language feature, you can replace that feature with the Language feature below.

#### Background Features

**Ability Scores.** When you determine your character’s ability scores, choose two of them, and increase one by 2 and the other one by 1. Alternatively, choose three ability scores, and increase each of them by 1.

**Skill Proficiencies.** Choose two Skills. Your character gains Proficiency in them.

**Tool Proficiency.** Choose one tool. Your character gains Tool Proficiency with it.

**Language.** Your character begins play knowing at least two languages: One language from the Standard Languages Table, and one language from either the Standard or Rare Languages tables. The character knows additional languages equal to their Intelligence ability score modifier at 1st level. A character does not learn languages retroactively when they raise their Intelligence ability score modifier.

**Feat.** Choose one Feat for which you meet the Prerequisites. Your character gains that Feat, but does not gain the *Ability Score Increase* part of it.

**Equipment.** Your character gains 50 GP to spend on starting equipment. The character keeps any unspent GP as spare coin.

# Species

## Aarakocra

A winged people who originated on the Elemental Plane of Air, aarakocra soar through the sky wherever they wander. They can stay aloft for hours, even days, locking their wings in place and letting warm updrafts carry them, and some flocks on their native plane fly for months, landing only to lay eggs and feed their young. The first aarakocra served mighty lords of the wind and were imbued with a measure of their masters' power over winds. Their descendants still command echoes of that power.

From below, aarakocra look like large birds and thus are sometimes called birdfolk. Only when they roost on a branch or walk across the ground is their humanoid nature clear. Standing upright, aarakocra are typically about 5 feet tall, thin and light, and they have long, narrow legs that taper to sharp talons. Feathers cover their bodies, usually red, orange, yellow, brown, or gray, and their heads often resemble those of parrots or eagles. They are fastidious about their plumage, they punctuate their speech with chirps to shade its meaning, and they cannot resist shiny, glittering objects. The idea of owning things baffles most of them. After all, who owns the sky?

Aarakocra live in small, insular colonies, usually a single large, open-roofed nest woven from vines, led by an elder with the help of a shaman. Confinement terrifies them, and being trapped underground or imprisoned by cold earth is a torment few can bear. They are sworn foes of elemental earth, and their word for gargoyle is loosely translated as "flying rock." Few aarakocra leave their roosts, and it takes a dire threat to their people, a mission of vengeance, or a catastrophe to draw one into the life of an adventurer.

| d6  | Why You Left the Roost                                                         |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1   | A gargoyle raid scattered your colony, and you hunt the ones responsible.      |
| 2   | You are the scout, sent to learn why the winds have turned wrong.              |
| 3   | A shiny relic carried you away from your people before you could stop yourself. |
| 4   | Your nest tumbled through a portal into a strange world, and you cannot find the way back. |
| 5   | You were exiled for breaking a vow, and the sky no longer feels like yours.    |
| 6   | You are searching for the lost pieces of an ancient wind-forged weapon.        |

\column

#### Traits
**Age.** Aarakocra reach maturity by age 3. They have relatively short lifespans compared to many other races, typically living to around 30 years old.  
**Size.** Your size is Medium.  
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet, and you have a flying speed equal to your walking speed.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Humanoid.  
**Flight. *(10 points)*** Because of your wings, you have a flying speed equal to your walking speed. You can't use this flying speed if you're wearing medium or heavy armor.  
**Talons. *(2 points)*** You have talons that you can use to make unarmed strikes. When you hit with them, the strike deals 1d6 + your Strength modifier slashing damage, instead of the bludgeoning damage normal for an unarmed strike.  
**Wind Caller. *(6 points)***  Starting at 3rd level, you can cast the *gust of wind* and *warding wind* spell with this trait, without requiring a material component. Once you cast a spell with this trait, you can't cast the same spell again with it until you finish a long rest. You can also cast these spells using any spell slots you have of 2nd level or higher.  
Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for when you cast a spell with this trait (choose when you select this species).

## Aasimar

Whether descended from a celestial being or infused with heavenly power, aasimar are mortals who carry a spark of the Upper Planes within their souls. They can fan that spark to bring light, ease wounds, and unleash the fury of the heavens. Many descend from a rare union between a mortal and a celestial, while others were ordinary mortals touched by some extraordinary event, such as an encounter with a powerful celestial or a place infused with the energy of the Upper Planes.

Aasimar can arise among any population of mortals, and they resemble their parents. They live for up to 160 years, and they often have features that hint at their celestial heritage. These begin subtle and become more obvious when the aasimar gains the ability to reveal their full celestial nature. Aasimar are rare enough that one may never meet another of their kind.

Many aasimar are guided by a celestial presence, which speaks in dreams, visions, or quiet intuition. Such a guide is wise but not infallible. To a distant angel, saving a few villagers might matter less than defeating a great evil, and following its advice can mean hard choices. An aasimar is still a mortal with free will. Some embrace their calling as a protector who stands vigilant against darkness, or as a scourge who burns with the need to cleanse evil. Others come to see their gift as a curse and drift away from it, and some fall into shadow, using the same power for selfish or darker ends. The Aasimar Celestial Features table has examples you can choose or use as inspiration to create your own.

| d6  | Celestial Feature                                |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------|
| 1   | A dusting of metallic, white, or charcoal freckles |
| 2   | Metallic, luminous, or dark eyes                |
| 3   | Starkly colored hair                             |
| 4   | An unusual hue tinting your shadow               |
| 5   | A ghostly halo crowning your head                |
| 6   | Rainbows gleaming on your skin                   |

#### Traits
**Age.** Aasimar mature at the same rate as humans, but they can live up to 160 years.  
**Size.** Your size is Medium or Small. You choose the size when you select this species.    
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Humanoid.  
**Celestial Resistance. *(4 points)*** You have resistance to necrotic damage and radiant damage.  
**Darkvision. *(3 point)*** You can see in dim light within 60 feet of you as if it were bright light and in darkness as if it were dim light. You discern colors in that darkness only as shades of gray.  
\column
**Healing Hands. *(3
points)*** As an action, you can touch a creature and roll a number of d4s equal to your proficiency bonus. The creature regains a number of hit points equal to the total rolled + your proficiency bonus. Once you use this trait, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest.  
**Light Bearer. *(2 points)*** You know the *light* cantrip. Charisma is your spellcasting ability for it.  
**Celestial Revelation. *(6 points)*** When you reach 3rd level, choose one of the revelation options below. Thereafter, you can use a bonus action to unleash the celestial energy within yourself, gaining the benefits of that revelation. Your transformation lasts for 1 minute or until you end it as a bonus action. Once you transform using your revelation below, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest.
- **Necrotic Shroud.** Your eyes briefly become pools of darkness, and ghostly, flightless wings sprout from your back temporarily. Creatures other than your allies within 10 feet of you that can see you must succeed on a Charisma saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Charisma modifier) or become frightened of you until the end of your next turn. Until the transformation ends, once on each of your turns, you can deal extra necrotic damage to one target when you deal damage to it with an attack or a spell. The extra damage equals your proficiency bonus.
- **Radiant Consumption.** Searing light temporarily radiates from your eyes and mouth. For the duration, you shed bright light in a 10-foot radius and dim light for an additional 10 feet, and at the end of each of your turns, each creature within 10 feet of you takes radiant damage equal to your proficiency bonus. Until the transformation ends, once on each of your turns, you can deal extra radiant damage to one target when you deal damage to it with an attack or a spell. The extra damage equals your proficiency bonus.
- **Radiant Soul.** Two luminous, spectral wings sprout from your back temporarily. Until the transformation ends, you have a flying speed equal to your walking speed, and once on each of your turns, you can deal extra radiant damage to one target when you deal damage to it with an attack or a spell. The extra damage equals your proficiency bonus.

## Artificial Hybrid

Artificial hybrids are creatures made by magical experiments, with the traits of animals fused into a living humanoid host. They begin as adult humans, elves, or other humanoids, who then undergo a process of magic and bioengineering and gain animal enhancements, such as ray-like fins, climbing talons, or acid-spitting glands.

Hybrids are made, not born, and they have no cohesive society of their own. Some were willing volunteers, and others were abducted and experimented on. Many struggle with what they have become. Most are treated with suspicion by outsiders, and a hybrid who deserts its creators may be hunted by them. A hybrid usually keeps the name its family gave it, though some choose new names after the change.

#### Traits
**Age.** Hybrids begin their lives as adult humans, elves or another humanoid species. They age at a slightly accelerated rate, so their maximum life spans are probably reduced somewhat.  
**Size.** Your size is Medium, within the normal range of your humanoid base species.  
**Speed. *(2 points)*** Your base walking speed is 35 feet.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Humanoid.  
**Darkvision. *(3 points)*** You can see in dim light within 60 feet of you as if it were bright light, and in darkness as if it were dim light. You can't discern color in darkness, only shades of gray.  

![hybrid](https://i.imgur.com/cu0RYFA.png) {position:absolute;bottom:30px;right:360px;width:470px;mix-blend-mode:multiply}

\column

**Animal Enhancement. *(4 points - 1st level) & (4 points - 3rd level) & (6 points - 5th level)*** Your body has been altered to incorporate certain animal characteristics. You choose one animal enhancement now, a second enhancement at 3rd level and a third enhancement at 5th level.
At 1st and 3rd level, choose one of the following options:

- **Manta Glide.** You have ray-like fins that you can use as wings to slow your fall or allow you to glide. When you fall and aren't incapacitated, you can subtract up to 100 feet from the fall when calculating falling damage, and you can move up to 2 feet horizontally for every 1 foot you descend.

- **Nimble Climber.** You have a climbing speed equal to your walking speed. In addition, at 3rd level, you can move up, down, and across vertical surfaces and upside down along ceilings, while leaving your hands free.

- **Underwater Adaptation.** You can breathe air and water, and you have a swimming speed equal to your walking speed.

At 5th level, your body evolves further, developing new characteristics. Choose one of the following options:

- **Grappling Appendage.** You have two special appendages growing alongside your arms. Choose whether they're both claws or tentacles. As an action, you can use one of them to try to grapple a creature. Each one is also a natural weapon, which you can use to make an unarmed strike. If you hit with it, the target takes bludgeoning damage equal to 1d6 + your Strength modifier, instead of the bludgeoning damage normal for an unarmed strike. Immediately after hitting, you can try to grapple the target as a bonus action. These appendages can't precisely manipulate anything and can't wield weapons, magic items, or other specialized equipment.

- **Carapace.** Your skin in places is covered by a thick shell. You gain a +1 bonus to AC when you're not wearing heavy armor.

- **Acid Spit.** When you take the Attack action on your turn, you can replace one of your attacks with a spray of acid from glands in your mouth, targeting one creature or object you can see within 30 feet of you. The target must make on a Dexterity saving throw against a DC equal to 8 + your Constitution modifier + your proficiency bonus. On a failed save, the creature or object takes 2d12 + your Constitution modifier acid damage. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage. This damage increases by 1d12 when you reach 11th level (3d12) and 17th level (4d12). You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses of it when you finish a long rest.

## Autognome

Autognomes are mechanical beings built by rock gnomes in their own image, usually with a particular purpose in mind. A gnome might build an autognome to be a steadfast colleague, a loyal companion, or a helper in the workshop. Sometimes, because of a malfunction or a unique circumstance, an autognome becomes separated from its creator and strikes out on its own.

An autognome bears a resemblance to its creator, and most autognomes are programmed to speak and understand Gnomish. The internal components used in an autognome's manufacture can vary wildly. One autognome might have an actual beating heart in its chest cavity, while another might be powered by stardust or intricate clockwork gears. Many are built of metal, but some are built of wood, stone, or whatever else was lying around. Autognomes are more "alive" than the typical construct, and they can benefit from healing magic that normally doesn't affect constructs.

Autognomes were built to serve, and many have programming that urges them to follow orders and help others. Sentience is hardly an exact science, though, and some have gone rogue, or simply have quirky programming. Like gnomes, autognomes can live for centuries, typically up to 500 years. Roll on the Autognome History table or choose an entry that you like to identify what event set you on the path to adventure. If nothing on the table appeals to you, work with your DM to create an origin story for your character.

| d6  | Story                                                               |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1   | Your creator gave you autonomy and urged you to follow your dreams. |
| 2   | Your creator died, leaving you to fend for yourself.                |
| 3   | A glitch caused you to forget your original programming.             |
| 4   | You didn't like how you were being treated by your creator, so you ran away from home. |
| 5   | You were built to complete a special mission.                        |
| 6   | You felt trapped in the role for which you were built and abandoned your creator, determined to find a greater purpose. |

\column

#### Traits
**Age.** Autognomes are mechanical constructs and do not age in the same way biological beings do. They are built rather than born and are considered fully functional (or "mature") from the moment of their creation. They can potentially function for centuries, provided they receive proper maintenance and repairs, as they do not suffer from aging effects like biological creatures.  
**Size.** Your size is Small.  
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Construct.  
**Armored Casing.** You are encased in thin metal or some other durable material. While you aren't wearing armor, your base Armor Class is 13 + your Dexterity modifier.  
**Built for Success.** You can add a d4 to one attack roll, ability check, or saving throw you make, and you can do so after seeing the d20 roll but before the effects of the roll are resolved. You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## Bariaur

## Bugbear

Neither bugs nor bears, bugbears are the hulking cousins of goblins and hobgoblins. With roots in the Feywild, early bugbears resided in hidden places, in hard-to-reach and shadowed spaces. Long ago and from out of the corner of your eye, they came to the Material Plane, urged to spread throughout the multiverse by a conquering goblinoid god. Centuries later, they still bear a fey gift for lurking just out of sight, and many of them have sneaked away from that god's influence.

Bugbears stand between 6 and 8 feet tall and weigh 250 to 350 pounds. They are long of limb and covered in coarse hair, with wedge-shaped ears and pointed teeth. Despite their formidable build, bugbears are quiet skulkers, thanks to a fey magic that lets them hide in spaces that seem far too small for them. They prefer to attack at night, relying on stealth and strength to strike before their prey knows they are there.

Between ambushes, bugbears spend much of their time resting or dozing, and they bully weaker creatures into doing their work. They are notorious for taking hostages, and some relish the chance to imprison and terrorize others. Foes who wander into bugbear territory often vanish without a trace. A bugbear adventurer might be a deserter from a warband, a rogue who found a better use for stealth, or a skulker who simply found the sleepy life in a party's wagon more comfortable.

## Changeling

With ever-changing appearances, changelings reside in many societies undetected. Each changeling can supernaturally adopt any face they like. For some changelings, a new face is only a disguise. For other changelings, a new face may reveal an aspect of their soul. They are talented infiltrators, diplomats, and tricksters, and there are likely changelings blending into the crowds of every city, though you would never know it.

The first changelings in the multiverse appeared in the Feywild, a land of extreme and fleeting emotion where many are mercurial and fickle, shifting easily from one mood, opinion, or identity to the next. The wondrous, mutable essence of that plane lingers in changelings today, even in those who have never set foot in the fey realm. In their true form, changelings appear faded, their features almost devoid of detail. It is rare to see a changeling in that form, for a typical changeling changes their shape the way others might change clothes.

A casual shape, created on the spur of the moment with no depth or history, is called a mask. A mask can express a mood or serve a specific purpose and then might never be used again. Many changelings, however, craft whole personas with histories, beliefs, and contacts of their own, with different personas for negotiation, investigation, and combat. Personas can be shared by multiple changelings, or passed down through a family, allowing a younger changeling to take advantage of the contacts the persona's previous users established. This can make a changeling's true personality hard to pin down, even for the changeling themself.

## Dwarf

Bold and hardy, dwarves are known as skilled warriors, miners, and workers of stone and metal. Though they stand well under 5 feet tall, dwarves are so broad and compact that they can weigh as much as a human standing nearly two feet taller. Their courage and endurance are easily a match for any of the larger folk. Dwarven skin ranges from deep brown to a paler hue tinged with red, but the most common shades are light brown or deep tan, like certain tones of earth. Their hair, worn long but in simple styles, is usually black, gray, or brown, though paler dwarves often have red hair. Male dwarves value their beards highly and groom them carefully.

Dwarven society is built around the clan. A dwarf's name is granted by a clan elder in accordance with tradition, and every proper dwarven name has been used and reused down through the generations. A dwarf's name belongs to the clan, not to the individual. A wrong done to one dwarf is a wrong done to the dwarf's entire clan, so what begins as one dwarf's hunt for vengeance can become a full-blown clan feud. Dwarves are known for their long memories and their love of fine craft, of gold and jewels, and of deep stonework. Their kingdoms usually lie deep beneath mountains and hills, where they mine gems and precious metals and forge items of wonder.

Dwarves are slow to trust and slower to forgive, and it takes a long time for a short-lived people such as humans to earn a dwarf's trust. Dwarves value honor, tradition, and loyalty to kin above wealth, and they find the quick, unpredictable ways of elves baffling. Many stubbornly defend what remains of their old halls, and some seek to reclaim what they have lost to the ravages of war and time. A dwarf adventurer might be seeking a lost ancestral weapon, a restored name, or a chance to prove themself beyond the clan.

## Elf

Elves are a magical people of otherworldly grace, living in the world but not entirely part of it. They trace their roots to the Feywild, and they live in places of ethereal beauty, in the midst of ancient forests or in silvery spires glittering with faerie light, where soft music drifts through the air and gentle fragrances waft on the breeze. Elves do not sleep. They enter a trance, a kind of wakeful meditation, in which they often relive earlier experiences and remain conscious of their surroundings. Elves tend to be chaotic and free-spirited. Some live in isolated enclaves, others walk among the shorter-lived peoples, and many find themselves caught between the two worlds. An elf adventurer might be chasing a vanished memory, fleeing a stifling, ageless society, or simply curious about what the world has made while the elves were not looking.

### Avariel

Avariel are the winged elves, among the most ancient of the elven peoples. Surface-dwelling drow carry the weight of their people's history, and others often see only the monster of legend rather than the individual. Some drow societies serve other gods entirely, and a few believe even a drow can be redeemed.

## Genasi

Tracing their ancestry to the genies of the Elemental Planes, each genasi can tap into the power of one of the elements. Air, earth, fire, and water are the four pillars of the Material Plane and the four types of genasi. Some genasi are direct descendants of a genie, others were born to non-genasi parents who lived near a place suffused by a genie's magic, and a rare few have a genie further up the family tree, manifesting an elemental heritage that lay dormant for generations. Genies can adopt mortal guise and travel incognito. During these visits, a mortal might catch a genie's eye, and friendship forms, romance blooms, and sometimes children result.

Seen in silhouette, a genasi can usually pass for human. Those of earth or water descent tend to be heavier, while those of air or fire tend to be lighter. Many genasi face a hard life, outcasts among both mortals and genies, and they seek isolation in the wilds, in mountains and forests, near lakes, or underground. Others rise to positions of great influence, especially where elemental beings are revered, and a few leave the Material Plane for the households of their genie parents. Genasi often take the names of whatever culture they are born into or wander into, though some take elemental names such as Stone, Smoke, Gust, Char, or Droplet. A typical genasi has a lifespan of about 120 years.

## Goblin

A subterranean folk, goblins can be found in every corner of the multiverse, often beside their bugbear and hobgoblin kin. Long before a conquering god bent them to his will, early goblins served in the court of a powerful archfey of the Feywild, a queen of air and darkness. Goblins thrived in her dangerous domain thanks to a special boon from her, a supernatural knack for finding the weak spots in foes larger than themselves and for getting out of trouble. Goblins brought this fey boon with them to worlds across the Material Plane, even if they don't remember the fey realm they inhabited before that god's rise.

Goblins stand about 3 to 4 feet tall, with pointed ears, large eyes, and wide mouths. Within goblinoid society, they occupy the lowest status, and even within goblin hierarchy, a caste system places lesser goblins in service to greater ones. Outside it, goblins are self-aware enough to know they are weaker than many other creatures, and they survive through cleverness, cunning, and sheer persistence. Now many goblins pursue their own destinies, escaping the plots of both archfey and gods.

## Goliath

At the highest mountain peaks, far above the slopes where trees grow and the air is thin and the frigid winds howl, dwell the reclusive goliaths. They wander a bleak realm of rock, wind, and cold. Distantly related to giants and infused with the supernatural essence of their ancestors' mountainous home, goliaths stand between 7 and 8 feet tall, with bodies that look as if they were carved from mountain stone and give them great physical power. They have a wide array of skin tones resembling different types of stone, often marked with bare patches and lighter or darker splotches.

Goliath life is a creed of fair competition. Their tribes of forty or fifty live in the harshest places, and they live by a simple rule: everyone must pull their own weight. They compete in games, contests, and challenges, with the winner earning the right to claim their place in the group, and many goliath cultures treat contests as binding agreements. A goliath keeps a score and tells the truth, and they value honor, strength, and fair play over comfort. They tend to keep to themselves, sticking to their own tight-knit clan groups, and they rarely leave their remote, hostile homelands.

Goliaths live less than a century, and their competitive way of life often makes it shorter. A goliath adventurer might be a champion seeking a worthy challenge, an outcast who failed to carry their weight, or a scout sent out to learn what the lowlanders are up to.

## Grung

Grungs are small, frog-like humanoids of the rain forests and jungles, with smooth, rubbery skin in vivid colors. They hatch from eggs and spend their first months as tadpoles. Grung poison has little effect on the undead, so grung communities tend to rely on outsiders to deal with ghouls and zombies.

## Hadozee

Hadozees reach adulthood at about age 20 and live roughly 200 years, though most active hadozees are young, as they retire to their jungle homes afterwards. A hadozee adventurer might be searching for magical secrets, the lore of fallen empires, or hidden treasure, or might simply be unable to see a horizon without wanting to know what is beyond it.

## Half-Troll

Half-trolls are rare hybrids whose looks vary with their parentage. They usually show their troll heritage in greenish skin, elongated noses, knobby growths on the skin, clawed hands, and unusually long limbs. These traits are strongest in half-trolls descended from giants, humanoids, or monstrous humanoids.

Trolls are ravenous giants, known for their hunger, their savagery, and their regeneration. A half-troll does not inherit that regeneration in full, but has a faster healing factor than most folk. Half-trolls are rare because trolls usually breed only among their own kind. Some half-trolls arise from captives bred to create powerful minions, from polymorphed creatures, or from lone trolls driven by instinct to mate with anyone they meet. Many half-trolls are raised among their non-troll parent's people, where their size, strength, and appearance set them apart. Some of them become adventurers out of a need to find a place where they belong.

## Halfling

The comforts of home are the goals of most halflings' lives: a place to settle in peace and quiet, far from marauding monsters and clashing armies; a blazing fire and a generous meal; fine drink and fine conversation. Though some halflings live out their days in remote agricultural communities, others form nomadic bands that travel constantly, lured by the open road and the wide horizon to discover the wonders of new lands and peoples. But even these wanderers love peace, food, hearth, and home, though home might be a wagon jostling along a dirt road or a raft floating downriver.

Halflings are small, practical, and affable. They cherish the bonds of family and friendship as well as the comforts of hearth and home, harboring few dreams of gold or glory. They have a knack for finding the most straightforward solution to a problem and little patience for dithering. They dress in simple, comfortable clothes in bright colors, and they have a rich tradition of storytelling, so much so that halflings often unwittingly keep lore about long-forgotten cultures and empires. A halfling has a given name, a family name, and possibly a nickname, and family names are often nicknames that stuck so tenaciously they were passed down through the generations. Some halflings are driven by curiosity, a kind word, or a bit of bad luck to leave their burrows behind and become adventurers.

Where wishing fails, ancient magic can offer a heart's desire, at least for a time. ## Hobgoblin

Hobgoblins trace their origins to the ancient courts of the Feywild, where they first appeared with their goblin and bugbear kin. On some worlds, such bonds lead hobgoblins to form communities with deep ties to one another, and vast hobgoblin legions have emerged, with ranks of devoted soldiers famed for their unity.

Hobgoblins are generally taller than their goblin cousins but not quite as big as bugbears, standing between 5 and 6 feet tall and weighing 150 to 200 pounds. They have curved, pointed ears and noses that turn bright red or blue during displays of emotion. War is the lifeblood of hobgoblins, and its glories are the dreams that inspire them. Hobgoblin society is built on fidelity to a rigid, unforgiving code of conduct, and they tend toward lawfulness. They are disciplined, tactical, and proud, and they take debts of honor very seriously. A hobgoblin adventurer might be a deserter, a veteran looking for a new cause, or a soldier whose legion fell.

## Human

In the reckonings of most worlds, humans are the youngest of the common species, late to arrive on the world scene and short-lived in comparison to dwarves, elves, and dragons. Perhaps it is because of their shorter lives that they strive to achieve as much as they can in the years they are given. Or maybe they feel they have something to prove to the elder species, and that's why they build their mighty empires on the foundation of conquest and trade. Whatever drives them, humans are the innovators, the achievers, and the pioneers of the worlds.

Humans are found throughout the multiverse and are as varied as they are numerous. They are fond of sprinkling their speech with words borrowed from other tongues, such as orc curses, elvish musical expressions, or dwarvish military phrases. Humans do not carry the weight of ancient prophecies or inherent magical bloodlines, and that freedom is what makes them so ambitious and so adaptable.

## Plasmoid

Plasmoids are amorphous beings with no typical shape. In the presence of other folk, they often adopt a similar shape, but there's little chance of mistaking a plasmoid for anything else. They consume food by osmosis, the way an amoeba does, and excrete waste through tiny pores. They breathe by absorbing oxygen through another set of pores, and their limbs are strong and flexible enough to grasp and manipulate weapons and tools. Although most plasmoids are translucent gray, they can alter their color and translucence by absorbing dyes through their pores.

Plasmoids don't have internal organs of the usual sort. Their bodies are composed of cells, fibers, plasma-like ooze, and clusters of nerves, which enable a plasmoid to detect light, heat, texture, sound, pain, and vibrations. They can stiffen the outer layers of their bodies to maintain a humanlike shape, so they can wear clothing and accessories, and they speak by forcing air out of tubular cavities that constrict to produce sound. When plasmoids sleep, they lose their rigidity and spread out, and are sometimes mistaken for a rock or some other feature of the environment. Plasmoids live up to about 130 years, and they have a curiosity about how other peoples live, with no fixed culture of their own to hold them back. A plasmoid adventurer might be an explorer, a student of other cultures, or someone who simply likes to slip through the cracks.

## Reborn

Death isn't always the end. The reborn exemplify this, being individuals who have died yet, somehow, still live. Some reborn exhibit the scars of fatal ends, their ashen flesh or bloodless veins making it clear that they've been touched by death. Other reborn are marvels of magic or science, being stitched together from disparate beings or bearing mysterious minds in manufactured bodies. Whatever their origins, reborn know a new life and seek experiences and answers all their own. To most common folk, very little distinguishes a reborn from the mindless constructs or evil undead of tales of adventure or horror, though a reborn keeps their free will.

Reborn suffer from some manner of discontinuity, an interruption of their lives or physical state that their minds are ill-equipped to deal with. Their memories of events before this interruption are often vague or absent, and the most unexpected experiences might cause sensations or visions of the past to come rushing back. Rather than sleeping, reborn regularly sit and dwell on the past, hoping for some revelation of what came before. Many are somber or pensive, with thoughts that grow muddled and confused. A reborn adventurer might be piecing together who they were, searching for what brought them back, or simply trying to build a life that is entirely their own.

Originating in the Feywild, a realm of pure emotion, satyrs thrive on the energy of merriment. They resemble elves but have goatlike legs, cloven hooves, and ram or goat horns. The magic of the fey realm has given them an innate ability to perform, to delight, and to resist magical intrusion. They have a well-earned reputation for their good spirits, gregarious personalities, and love of revels. Most satyrs are driven by simple desires: to see the world and to sample its every pleasure. They believe that other peoples are woefully burdened with the plague of seriousness.

For satyrs, revelry is a way of life, rooted in the delight of small things: the song of a bird, a warm breeze, the smell of a tasty pie, relaxing by a river in the sunshine. Satyrs quickly bury negative thoughts, and the loss of a friend leads them only to redouble their efforts to make more. While satyrs are usually found in the Feywild, they do wander to other planes, most often to the Material Plane, where they seek to bring a bit of their home plane's splendor to other worlds. Satyrs can be fierce when their home is threatened, fighting as skirmishers and then melting back into the forest. Every satyr knows at least one instrument, and the melodies they play are inherently magical.

## Sahuagin

Across fog-shrouded coasts and endless ocean swells, an ominous drone from a conch shell chills the blood of all who hear it: sahuagin are coming. These fearsome aquatic humanoids are hunters and raiders, with scaled bodies, webbed hands and feet, rows of sharp teeth, and large eyes made for the deep ocean's darkness. Their scales are typically green or blue, darker on the back and paler on the belly, and they adorn themselves with shell, coral, and the bones of their enemies.

Sahuagin society is an orderly and ruthless meritocracy. Titles are earned through prowess and lost through weakness, and showing weakness is to risk death. Their creed holds that one should always be the hunter and never the hunted. They worship Sekolah, a great shark god, and feed living sacrifices to the sharks that follow their priests. They also command sharks, and often fight alongside them. Rare sahuagin are born with unusual mutations, including extra limbs. In some tales, malenti are born with a more elven look and a gentler nature, and a few of them leave the sea's hunting grounds behind. A sahuagin adventurer might be a malenti, an exile, or a rare member of the people who found the other creeds of the sea more appealing than their own.

## Shifter

Shifters are sometimes called weretouched, as they are descendants of people who contracted full or partial lycanthropy. Humanoids with a bestial aspect, shifters can't fully change shape, but they can temporarily enhance their animalistic features by entering a state they call shifting. They are similar to humans in height and build but are typically more lithe and flexible. Their facial features have a bestial cast, often with large eyes and pointed ears, and most have prominent canine teeth. They grow fur-like hair on nearly every part of their bodies.

Early in childhood, a shifter forms a bond with the beast within, a totemic force that shapes their body and mind. Some shifters believe these spirits are independent entities and speak of a Bear or a Wolf as an ancestor or guide. Beasthide shifters are stoic, stubborn, and thick-skinned. Longtooth shifters are fierce and aggressive, but form deep bonds with their friends and prefer to run in a pack. Swiftstride shifters are graceful, quick, and often aloof. Wildhunt shifters are sharp, insightful hunters, and many become the spiritual leaders of their communities. Many civilized folk fear shifters for what their ancestors were, so shifters tend to be self-sufficient, resourceful, and wary of those who are quick to judge. Most resemble a particular kind of lycanthrope. You can choose the kind in your past, or roll on the Lycanthrope Ancestor table.

## Siren

Sirens are enchanting fey who dwell in coastal waters, rivers, and the deep places of the sea. They are renowned for the songs that draw sailors and travelers to their doom. They are often mistaken for mermaids, but they are more dangerous, and far rarer. Their striking beauty and their voices are as much a weapon as their claws and teeth. A siren's song can enchant, calm, or captivate, and sirens delight in using it to lead the unwary into the water.

Not every siren is a killer. Sirens are playful and fun-loving among their own kind, and some use their voices only to entertain or to carry messages. A siren in good spirits may still carry ill news. A siren adventurer might be a wanderer who tired of the hunt, an exile from a flock, or a curious fey who wants to hear songs no one has sung before.

## Tabaxi

Tabaxi tend toward chaos, letting impulse and fancy guide them, and are rarely evil. A tabaxi adventurer is likely to be an incurable explorer, a collector of stories, or a thief of wonders.

## Thri-kreen

Thri-kreen have insectile features and two pairs of arms. Their bodies are encased in protective chitin, and they can alter the coloration of this carapace to blend in with their natural surroundings. Thri-kreen speak by clacking their mandibles and waving their antennae, indicating to other thri-kreen what they are thinking and feeling. Other creatures find this method of communication difficult to interpret and impossible to duplicate. To interact with other folk, thri-kreen rely on a form of telepathy.

Thri-kreen hatch from eggs, and they feel compelled to belong to a clutch, a small group of close companions who are protected as family while everyone else is treated as a stranger. When thri-kreen enter other societies, they still feel the pull of the clutch and often accept humanoids as clutchmates. There are no permanent thri-kreen communities. Most are nomadic hunters, sleepless wanderers who travel in small groups, and who are known for their tracking and fighting skills. Thri-kreen live roughly 30 years, and they are mature and contributing to the clutch by age 5. A thri-kreen adventurer might be a hunter who has adopted a new clutch, a loner whose old clutch is gone, or an explorer who has seen more than its kin can imagine.

## Tiefling

A tiefling's fiendish legacy promises power but has no effect on their moral outlook. Many tieflings meet mistrust, and each responds in their own way: by hiding it, flaunting it, or proving they are more than their blood. Tieflings mature like humans and live 10 to 20 years longer.

## Tortle

Tortles have a saying: "We wear our homes on our backs." These turtle folk live on many worlds, most often journeying up and down coasts, along waterways, and across the sea. Tortles don't have a unified story of how they were created, but they all have a sense of being mystically connected to the natural world. Carrying their shelter on their backs gives tortles a special feeling of security wherever they go, for even if they visit a far, unknown country, they have a place to lay their heads. Tortles exhibit the same range of coloration and patterns found among turtles, and many enjoy adorning their shells in distinctive ways.

A tortle settles near others for a time, making friends with its neighbors while respecting their privacy. At some point, usually five to twenty years in, a tortle feels an overwhelming urge to venture far from home and see the world. They may return to share what they have found and learned, only to wander again. Tortles are peaceful, nature-loving, and wise in the skills of survival, and they love to share tales with those they meet. What many tortles consider a simple life, others might call a life of adventure. Tortles reach adulthood by about age 15 and live an average of 250 years.

## Vedalken

Nothing is perfect. Vedalken not only believe this fact, they rejoice in it. Every imperfection is a chance for improvement, and progress is an endless march toward a state of perfection that can never be reached. This viewpoint leads vedalken to pursue their work with delighted enthusiasm, never deterred by setbacks and excited by every opportunity for improvement.

Vedalken are tall and slender, standing almost a head taller than humans on average but weighing about the same. Their hairless skin comes in a range of shades of blue. Their eyes are darker shades of blue or violet. They lack external ears, their noses are broad and flat, and they are partially amphibious.

Despite being talkative, vedalken keep their personal lives private, and they tend to engage more with ideas than with people. They form close friendships based on mutual interests or compelling disagreements, and their interactions dwell on their thoughts about those issues rather than their feelings about them. To members of other species, vedalken often seem cold, even emotionless. That assessment isn't fair. They feel emotion every bit as intensely as other folk do, but they are skilled at not displaying it. Cool rationality guides their actions, they make and follow careful plans, and they are patient enough to do nothing when the ideal outcome relies on such inaction. Their curious intellects incline vedalken toward scholarly orders, legal institutions, and schools of magical science, and they put their intelligence to use in crafting and improving things, whether those things are laws, procedures, or magical sciences. They live about 350 years, with some reaching 500.

#### Traits
**Age.** Vedalken mature slower than humans do. reaching maturity around age 40. Their life span is typically 350 years, with some living to the age of 500.  
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.  
**Size.** Tall and slender, Vedalken stand 6 to 6½ feet tall on average and usually weigh less than 200 pounds. Your size is Medium.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Humanoid.  
**Vedalken Dispassion. *(8 points)*** You have advantage on all Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saving throws.  
**Tireless Precision. *(9 points)*** You are proficient in two of the following skills of your choice: Arcana, History, Investigation, Medicine, Performance, or Sleight of Hand. You are also proficient with one tool of your choice.
Whenever you make an ability check with the chosen skill or tool, roll a d4 and add the number rolled to the check's total.  
**Partially Amphibious. *(1 point)*** By absorbing oxygen through your skin, you can breathe underwater for up to 1 hour. Once you've reached that limit, you can't use this trait again until you finish a long rest.

## Verdan

The verdan owe their existence to chaos. Descended and transformed from a large clan of goblins and hobgoblins, those who became the verdan were simply living their lives, doing goblinoid things. Then an unknown magical force of chaos changed them forever. Their skin turned the color of jade and their blood began to flow black. Their ears grew pointed, and they gained a limited form of telepathy, but at the cost of forgetting their history. The underground homes of the goblins were places of terror to the new creatures they had become, and the verdan quickly fled to the surface and into the sunlight.

When they arrived in the lands of the surface, the verdan found that they were often mistaken for smaller, green-skinned half-elves. Attracting curiosity but no real questions, they have been able to make their way reasonably freely in the world. Verdan are friendly, kind, and curious, and a bit naive, though their instincts for chaos tell them when they are being tricked, often a little too late. They are the newest people in the world, and they do their best to find their way in an unfamiliar one. A verdan starts out small, but each one eventually undergoes a sudden growth spurt of two feet or more, and no verdan has yet died of old age, so their true lifespan is a mystery.

#### Traits

**Age.** Verdan reach adulthood at around the age of 24, and it is thought that they might live to nearly 200 years old. However, because no verdan has died of old age since the species's initial creation, their upper age limits remain subject to speculation.  
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.  
**Size.** Verdan start out similar in size to the goblins they were created from, ranging from 3 to 4 feet in height. But at some point after reaching maturity, each verdan undergoes a sudden growth spurt of 2 feet or more. At 1st level, you are a Small creature. When you reach 5th level, you become a Medium creature.  
**Creature Type. *(0 points)*** You are a Humanoid. You are also considered a goblinoid for any prerequisite or effect that requires you to be a goblinoid.   
**Black Blood Healing. *(2 points)*** The black blood boosts your natural healing. When you roll a 1 or 2 on any Hit Die you spend at the end of a short rest, you can reroll the die and must use the new roll.  
**Limited Telepathy. *(5 points)*** You can telepathically speak to any creature you can see within 30 feet of you. You don't need to share a language with the creature for it to understand your telepathy, but it must be able to understand at least one language.   
**Persuasive. *(4 points)*** Your people's lack of history makes you trustworthy and humble. You have proficiency in the Persuasion and Deception skill.  
**Telepathic Insight. *(6 points)*** Your mind's connection to the world around you strengthens your will. You have advantage on all Wisdom and Charisma saving throws.

## Warforged

Warforged are formed from a blend of organic and inorganic materials. Root-like cords infused with alchemical fluids serve as their muscles, wrapped around a framework of steel, darkwood, or stone. Armored plates form a protective outer shell and reinforce joints. Warforged share a common facial design, with a hinged jaw and crystal eyes embedded beneath a reinforced brow ridge. Although they were manufactured, warforged are living humanoids. Resting, healing magic, and the Medicine skill all provide the same benefits to warforged that they do to other humanoids.

Warforged were built to serve and to fight, and for most of their existence, they had a clearly defined function and were encouraged to focus purely on that role. The typical warforged shows little emotion, and many embrace a concrete purpose, such as protecting allies, completing a contract, or exploring a land, much as they once embraced war. However, there are warforged who delight in exploring their feelings, their freedom, and their relationships with others. The more a warforged develops its individuality, the more likely it is to modify its body, seeking out an artificer to customize the look of its face, limbs, and plating.

#### Traits

**Age.** A typical warforged is between two and thirty years old. The maximum warforged lifespan remains a mystery; so far, warforged have shown no signs of deterioration due to age. You are immune to magical aging effects.  
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.  
**Size.** Your size is Medium.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Humanoid.  
**Constructed Resilience.** You were created to have remarkable fortitude, represented by the following benefits:
- You have advantage on saving throws against being poisoned, and you have resistance to poison damage.  
- You don't need to eat, drink, or breathe. 
- You are immune to disease.
- You don't need to sleep, and magic can't put you to sleep. 
___
**Sentry's Rest.** When you take a long rest, you must spend at least six hours in an inactive, motionless state, rather than sleeping. In this state, you appear inert, but it doesn't render you unconscious, and you can see and hear as normal.
___
**Integrated Protection.** Your body has built-in defensive layers, which can be enhanced with armor:
- You gain a +1 bonus to Armor Class.
- You can don only armor with which you have proficiency. To don armor other than a shield, you must incorporate it into your body over the course of 1 hour, during which you remain in contact with the armor. To doff armor, you must spend 1 hour removing it. You can rest while donning or doffing armor in this way.
- While you live, the armor incorporated into your body can't be removed against your will.
___
**Specialized Design.** You gain one skill proficiency and one tool proficiency of your choice.

## Yuan-Ti

Yuan-ti were originally humans who transformed themselves into serpent folk through ancient rituals. They are all that remains of an ancient, decadent empire of humans, whose rites of transformation granted them power, knowledge, and resistance to magic and poison. Most yuan-ti were corrupted into monsters by those rites, but some instead became a new people who mix characteristics of humans and snakes. Their empires have long since withered or been defeated, and the survivors live far from other peoples, in remote temples in jungles, swamps, and deserts, where many still dream of supplanting the rest of the world and making themselves gods.

Each yuan-ti manifests their serpentine heritage in a variety of ways: a forked tongue, snake eyes, a snakelike nose, or some other ophidian characteristic. Many are cold, calculating, and deeply patient, with emotions they dampen or conceal, and they tend to live by a hard-edged hierarchy. Yet however a yuan-ti looks, they have the power to pursue great good or evil in the multiverse, and some turn their backs on their kin's ambitions. A yuan-ti adventurer might be a spy for their temple, a defector, or a lone exile trying to prove they are more than the ritual that made them.

#### Traits
**Age.** Yuan-Ti hatch from eggs and reach adulthood by twelve years of age. An average lifespan for any yuan-ti is 80 years, but attaining an age as great as 120 years is possible.  
**Size.** Your size is Medium or Small. You choose the size when you select this species.  
**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.  
**Creature Type.** You are a Humanoid.  
**Darkvision. *(3 points)*** You can see in dim light within 60 feet of you as if it were bright light and in darkness as if it were dim light. You discern colors in that darkness only as shades of gray.  
**Magic Resistance. *(8 points)*** You have advantage on saving throws against spells.  
**Poison Resilience. *(5 points or 3 points with Magic Resistance)*** You have advantage on saving throws you make to avoid or end the poisoned condition on yourself. You also have resistance to poison damage.  
**Serpentine Spellcasting. *(5 points)*** You know the *poison spray* cantrip. You can cast *animal friendship* an unlimited number of times with this trait, but you can target only snakes with it. Starting at 5th level, you can also cast *suggestion* with this trait. Once you cast it, you can't do so again until you finish a long rest. You can also cast it using any spell slots you have of 2nd level or higher.

Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for these spells when you cast them with this trait (choose when you select this species).
